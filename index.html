Trees Hidden in Integers

Every natural number encodes a unique rooted tree. This is Matula's bijection: 1 maps to a single node, primes extend depth, composites add children. These visualizations explore what this structure reveals about number theory.

The Geometry of Rationals

Binary trees enumerate all positive rationals. The same tree appears as Stern-Brocot mediants, Calkin-Wilf iteration, and continued fraction paths. Ford circles visualize this structure as tangent circles. At the deepest level, it's all hyperbolic geometry: Ford circles are horocycles, the modular group acts by isometries, and the pattern tiles the Poincaré half-plane.

Ford Circles

Every rational p/q gets a circle of radius 1/(2q²). Two circles are tangent iff the rationals are Farey neighbors. Geometry from arithmetic.

Hyperbolic Geometry

The unifying perspective. Ford circles are horocycles in the Poincaré half-plane. The modular group PSL(2,ℤ) tiles hyperbolic space with copies of a fundamental domain. Number theory and non-Euclidean geometry are the same subject.

Deep connection

The Unifying Thread

Stern-Brocot ↔ Calkin-Wilf ↔ Continued Fractions ↔ Ford Circles ↔ Hyperbolic Plane

These aren't separate topics. They're different views of the same mathematical object: the structure of rational numbers under mediant operations, encoded by the modular group.
